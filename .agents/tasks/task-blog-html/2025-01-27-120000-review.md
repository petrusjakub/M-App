# Standalone blog page for Manulife insurance content marketing

A single self-contained HTML file (`satuyangterpenting.wordpress.com.html`) containing 21 long-form blog articles in Bahasa Indonesia, targeted at selling Manulife insurance products and recruiting agents. The page uses an accordion UI to keep articles collapsed, includes click-to-copy functionality for each article, and drives readers toward a Workers site and WhatsApp contact. All CSS and JS are inlined — zero external dependencies.

Watch for: The `max-height: 5000px` accordion technique will clip articles that exceed that height on small-screen/large-font devices (confirmed). No `<meta og:*>` tags for social sharing despite the content being explicitly designed for social distribution (confirmed). Copyright year hardcoded to 2024 (confirmed).

## High-level view

The page is a marketing content hub — 21 emotional-storytelling articles spread across 7 days (3 per day at 07:00, 12:00, 19:00). Each article sits inside an accordion that toggles via inline JS. Schedule badges mark the intended publishing slot. Every article ends with two CTA buttons (web link + WhatsApp) and a set of SEO hashtags. A "Copy Artikel" button grabs article text and hashtags for pasting into social platforms.

The content mix alternates between product education (health insurance, unit link, whole life vs term life), emotional selling (medical emergency stories, family protection), business opportunity (income claims, team building, career change), and objection handling (claim process, myths) — intentional variety to sustain a 7-day drip campaign.

For a page whose entire purpose is social distribution, the absence of Open Graph and Twitter Card meta tags is a meaningful gap: link previews will have no image and generic text. The accordion's fixed `max-height: 5000px` ceiling could clip long articles on narrow viewports with large fonts. The clipboard function works correctly for article text and hashtags but includes trailing call-to-action sentences that reference links not present in the copied output.

## Accordion overflow and the 5000px ceiling

The expand/collapse mechanism uses a CSS `max-height` transition from `0` to `5000px`. If any article's rendered content exceeds 5000px in height — plausible on a 320px-wide device with large system fonts, given several articles are 800+ words with CTAs and hashtag blocks — the bottom will be clipped with no scroll and no visual indication of truncation. The transition timing is also fixed at `0.4s` regardless of content length, so long articles animate slowly while short ones snap open instantly. Raising the ceiling to a much larger value (e.g., `100000px`) or switching to JS-measured `scrollHeight` assignment would eliminate both issues.

## Missing social sharing metadata

The `<head>` includes `<meta name="description">`, `<meta name="keywords">`, and `<meta name="author">`. However, no Open Graph tags (`og:title`, `og:description`, `og:image`, `og:url`) or Twitter Card meta are present. When this URL is shared on WhatsApp, Facebook, or Twitter, the link preview will show the bare `<title>` text with no image. For clickbait-style emotional content that depends on compelling previews to drive clicks, this omission directly undermines the distribution strategy.

## Clipboard: trailing CTA context bleeds through

`copyToClipboard` selects all `<p>` nodes inside `.accordion-body` and concatenates their `textContent`, then appends hashtag spans. CTA button text (inside `<a>` elements) is correctly excluded. But the final prose paragraph before CTAs typically says something like "Kalau lu pengen tau lebih detail, langsung aja hubungi gue" or "Klik tombol di bawah" — references to UI elements that don't exist in the pasted output. A reader who pastes this into a WhatsApp broadcast or WordPress post will have a dangling call-to-action pointing nowhere. Adding a wrapper `<div class="article-prose">` around the copyable portion and stopping before CTAs would solve this cleanly.
